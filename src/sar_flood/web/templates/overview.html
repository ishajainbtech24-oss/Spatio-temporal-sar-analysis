Where radar sees water through cloud

Calm water reflects Sentinel-1's radar pulse away from the satellite, so it shows up as the darkest pixels in a SAR image — day or night, through cloud. This tool maps that water pixel by pixel on Sen1Floods11 chips and compares a learned model against classical thresholds.

Analyse a chip See evaluation

How a chip is processed

  1. Read a 2-band GeoTIFF (VV, VH in dB). No-data pixels stay masked all the way through.
  2. Despeckle with a NaN-aware 3×3 median filter.
  3. Scale backscatter with a fixed −50…1 dB range, so absolute brightness — the water cue — is kept.
  4. Segment with a U-Net whose encoder was first trained as an autoencoder on unlabelled training chips.
  5. Compare with Otsu and fixed −15 dB thresholds, and with ground truth when it is supplied.

Scope and limits

  • Labels mark all surface water in the post-event image, including rivers and lakes. Output is a water map; a flood map also needs a pre-event or permanent-water layer.
  • Each Sen1Floods11 hand-labelled chip is a single acquisition, so nothing here models change over time.
  • Only metrics written by sar-flood evaluate appear on the evaluation page.
